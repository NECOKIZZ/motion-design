# 02 · Terrain: the product's visual world

Every product lives in a terrain: a world of objects, actions, materials and sounds its audience already knows. A football product can use a ball, pitch lines, a whistle, a scoreboard. A privacy product can use redaction blocks, hashes, frosted glass. A prediction market can use YES/NO, odds tickers, a probability bar. When the video's props, transitions and textures all come from the terrain, every frame reads as *this* product, even with the logo covered. When they don't, you get the generic SaaS video: floating cards, gradient blobs, a phone with a notification.

This stage turns a product into a terrain table the rest of the storyboard draws from.

## 1. Find the terrain

Ask five questions and write short, concrete answers (nouns and verbs, not adjectives):

1. **Where does the product happen?** (a market stall, a stadium, a trading floor, a WhatsApp chat, a kitchen)
2. **What objects are there?** (ball, scoreboard, receipt, padlock, delivery box, naira notes, phone)
3. **What actions happen there?** (kick, whistle, stamp, seal, haggle, swipe, deliver, flip)
4. **What materials and light?** (floodlights and turf; paper and ink; frosted glass and neon; chrome and LED)
5. **What sounds?** (whistle, crowd swell, cash-register ding, message pop, stamp thud)

A product usually spans two terrains: its **domain** (football, money, food) and its **medium** (an app, a chat, a marketplace). Use both; the domain gives the metaphors, the medium gives the proof.

## 2. Turn the terrain into devices

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Object | A noun from the terrain |
| Motion it naturally does | Its native verb (a ball is kicked, a stamp thuds, a flap flips) |
| Device on screen | How it appears in a beat |
| Transition it can become | How it carries one scene into the next |
| Feature it can prove | Which feature or message it serves |

The **transition** column is where terrains pay off. A natural transition is an action the object already performs:

| Terrain | Object action | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| Football | ball kicked across frame | whip pan following the ball |
| Football | centre circle | iris from the kickoff spot |
| Football | scoreboard flap | split-flap flip of the whole frame |
| Football | halfway line | line wipe |
| Privacy | redaction bar | bar sweeps across and becomes the next scene's background |
| Privacy | hash string | characters scramble and resolve into the next headline |
| Privacy | frosted glass | focus pull: frame frosts, the new scene sharpens behind |
| Prediction market | probability bar | bar fills to 100% and floods the frame in the winner colour |
| Prediction market | YES/NO toggle | toggle slides; the frame slides with it |
| Payments | card swipe | card swipes through frame as a push |
| Payments | receipt | receipt prints downward, its paper becomes the next background |
| Commerce / delivery | parcel box | camera dives into the opening box (zoom through) |
| Commerce | shopping bag | bag drops through frame (gravity drop) |
| Chat / social | message bubble | bubble expands to fill the frame (card morph) |
| Music | vinyl / waveform | record spin becomes a clock wipe; waveform spike is the cut |
| Food | plate / lid | cloche lifts to reveal the next scene (vertical push) |
| Travel | boarding pass | pass tears along the perforation (split) |
| Health | heartbeat line | ECG spike draws across and becomes a wipe |
| AI / dev tools | cursor, prompt box | prompt submits; the response floods the frame |
| Real estate | door / window | push through a window into the next room |
| Education | page | page turn (3D flip) |

## 3. Sort strong props from generic ones

After the table, write two lists:

- **Signature props** (only this product could use): 2–4 items. These carry the signature moments.
- **Generic props to avoid or justify**: things every video in this category uses. For fintech: rising line graphs, floating credit cards, coins. For SaaS: dashboards with random charts, notification bells. For e-commerce: shopping carts, sale tags. You can still use one if it carries a proof moment, but never as decoration.

## 4. Check against the brand

- Does any terrain object clash with the brand's tone? (A playful cartoon ball in a premium betting brand.)
- Does any motif contradict the message? (Hiding motifs for a product about transparency.)
- Is the terrain culturally right for the audience? Prefer the audience's local objects: for a Nigerian marketplace, a market stall, a dispatch rider, a WhatsApp catalogue and naira notes beat a generic shopping cart.

## 5. Worked terrain: AvioMax (Nigerian marketplace)

| Object | Native motion | Device | Transition | Proves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market stall awning | unrolls | the store opening: a striped awning unrolls over the seller's storefront card | awning drops as a vertical wipe | "open a store" |
| Product link | is copied, pasted, travels | a red line with a link-chip head; pasted into IG, TikTok, WhatsApp at once | link-line wipe; or a chat bubble expands into the next scene (card morph) | sell on social |
| Verified stamp | thuds | stamp lands on a grey seller card, card turns accent | — | verified sellers |
| Naira note / sale | splits | ₦10,000 splits; ₦9,400 flies to the wallet, ₦600 peels away | wallet fills and pushes out | 6% commission |
| Dispatch box | is sealed and shipped | box tape seals, box slides off frame | box slides as a push | delivered |
| Paystack payment | card taps, seals | card number collapses into a sealed token | — | secure payments |

Signature props: the awning, the pasted link in three chats, the naira split.
Generic props to avoid: shopping cart icon, floating credit card, 3D coins, "sale" tags.

## 6. Output of this stage

The terrain table, the signature props, the generic-avoid list, and one line naming the terrain's **native transition** (the one you will most likely use as the primary): e.g. *"Native transition: the link-line wipe (the red store link sweeps across and reveals the next scene behind it)."*
