# 14 · The house look: dark premium, neon, glass, glints, real 3D

This is the look the user loves. Make it the default for every video unless the user asks for something else. It is a description to design from, not a template to fill: each film should feel like it belongs to the same family, with fresh ideas inside it.

The brand changes the colours, fonts and logo; the look stays. Take the brand's signature colour as the **accent**, and its colour for money, success or "go" as the **signal**. If the brand has no such colour, choose a bright one that sits far from the accent and glows on black.

## The feeling

Quiet, expensive, futuristic. A dark stage where light is precious, so every glow means something. Calm typography, confident motion, and real depth. It should feel like a premium tech launch, never like an ad shouting at you.

## The stage

- **A near-black background**, never flat. Soft vertical curtains of accent-coloured light drift slowly behind everything, like light through haze. A very faint grid fades out toward the edges, with a vignette and fine film grain on top.
- Light lives where the subject is: a soft accent glow blooms behind whatever is in focus, especially the 3D.
- Lots of negative space. One subject at a time, centred or clearly anchored, with darkness doing the framing.

## 3D comes first

Real 3D is a big part of why this look feels premium. Reach for it before flat graphics wherever it can carry meaning:
- **The logo in 3D.** Extrude the mark so it has thickness. It turns in from the side and locks into place on a strong beat. Any separate part of the mark (a dot, a ball, an accent) can fly on its own and dock back into the logo; that docking is the lock.
- **The logo face stays flat and exactly the logo colour.** No shine, gradient or gleam on the face, because it must read as the real mark. All the depth and light live on the extruded sides, in dark glossy metal that catches the accent light.
- **A signature 3D sculpture** built from the product's own idea (for example, fine concentric metal rings for a product about closeness). It opens the film rising out of the dark, returns at the end, and can become a set the camera moves over, with objects placed on it.
- **Glossy metal in the accent colour**, lit by an accent key light, a cool white rim light and an environment reflection, so edges catch moving highlights.
- **Living 3D**: slow waves, gentle wobble, a ripple when something lands. It is never frozen and never busy.
- **3D objects carry the story.** Things land in the 3D world, become the transitions, and match-cut into the UI (a card collapses into the ball, the ball reappears in 3D).
- Camera moves like a real camera: low hero angles for the sculpture, a rise to look down on a field, a slow push in on holds.

## Glass, light and glints

- **Product UI as dark glass**: translucent smoked panels, a strong backdrop blur over the light curtains, hairline borders with a brighter top edge and deep soft shadows. The real product, rebuilt and working: buttons press, values change, sliders slide.
- **One light pass** sweeps across a glass surface when it lands, with a **star glint** popping where the light exits a corner or catches a 3D edge. Glints are rare and timed, like sparkles on the beat.
- Selected states glow softly in the accent.

## Neon signal

- The signal colour is the payoff, and it appears **only** for the payoff and the call to action, so the viewer learns that this colour means "you win".
- On the biggest musical drop, the frame floods with a deep, glowing version of the signal colour, and huge numerals count up in neon with a real glow around them.
- The call to action is a solid signal-coloured button with a pressable lip, pressed on the last hit.

## Type

- Small, calm, centred captions in a clean display face, sentence case. Roughly 40–56px at 1080p, at most one line and about six words.
- One accent word per caption in a serif italic, lightly tinted with the accent.
- Big type only for numbers that matter (counters, payoffs).
- Words blur up into place quickly, word by word, and leave quickly. They never compete with the 3D, and never enter during a transition.

## Signature beats that worked

The dark open with the sculpture rising; an object falling into it with a ripple; the 3D logo turning in and locking; the glass product card rising out of the dark; real UI interactions on the beat; a card collapsing into an object that continues in 3D; the camera rising over a 3D field; the signal flood with neon numbers on the drop; a list cascading in; the 3D lockup, the end line and the CTA press to close.

## Where to push further next time

The user approved the look. What they want better is **smoothness, transitions, music sync and movement**. When designing in this look:
- Make moves snap and settle, then hold briefly (≤ 1.5s); avoid floaty in-and-out easing on everything.
- Pace: a scene per bar for one idea (a phrase for a demo or the hero), arrivals on pulses, holds sized to reading (`15-pace-and-flow.md`). The look stays calm through its type and light, not through long pauses. The same film felt stalled at 60s and still laggy at 38s next to a flat 30s cut that moved bar by bar; 3D and glass may move up to about 1.5× slower than flat UI, not 3×.
- Keep one continuous camera feel, so 3D and UI hand off through a move rather than a hide-and-show.
- Carry every cut on an object.
- Choose music with real structure (stops, hits, drops) and land the big moments on it.
- Overlap scene handoffs so no frame is left waiting.
