# 03 · Concept, spine and signature moments

A storyboard without a concept is a slideshow: six scenes that each look fine and add up to nothing. The concept is the one idea that makes the video feel authored.

## 1. Five pitches, two of them unlikely

Before writing any beat, answer four questions specifically for this brief:

1. **What does the subject look like?** (from the terrain)
2. **What does the target emotion look like as a frame?** Relief is a cluttered frame that suddenly empties. Urgency is compression and fast cuts. Trust is stillness, one object, plenty of space. Excitement is scale: one thing too big for the frame.
3. **What does the platform demand?** A feed fights for the first second; a site hero loops; a pitch deck video can breathe.
4. **What does every other video in this category look like?** That is the anti-pattern.

Then write five concepts, one from each source: the terrain · the emotion · the audience (meet or break their expectation) · the anti-pattern inverted · an unusual format (a receipt, a front page, a countdown, a map, a chat thread, a single continuous camera move). Estimate privately how likely another model would be to produce each; at least two must be unlikely (under ~10%). Two concepts with the same silhouette (the same rough layout of shapes) are one concept; replace one.

Present each pitch in three lines: the concept in one sentence · its visual world · its opening hook. Recommend one and say why. The user may mix.

## 2. The spine

The spine is the single device that threads every beat, so the video feels like one continuous piece rather than separate scenes. Name it before writing beats.

| Spine type | Example |
|---|---|
| A persistent object | the product link travels through every scene; each feature is a place it lands |
| A persistent frame | everything happens inside one phone that the camera orbits, enters, exits |
| A continuous camera | one unbroken camera move across a huge canvas; "scenes" are stations on it |
| A recurring shape | a circle that is a coin, then a stamp, then a notification dot, then the logo |
| A colour rule | the accent colour only appears on things the product causes |
| A counter | one number (orders, ₦ earned, match minute) that climbs across the whole video |
| A recurring sound + motion | every proof moment ends in the same stamp thud |

A good spine makes transitions easy: if a shape persists, the transition is that shape moving.

## 3. Signature moments

Pick 1–2 moments that only this product could have, built from signature props (see `02-terrain.md`). These get the boldest motion, the strongest music hits, and the most build time. Everything else supports them.

Write each as one sentence with a verb: *"A ₦10,000 note splits in two on the drop; ₦9,400 flies into the seller's wallet and the wallet bulges."*

## 4. Rhythm shape

Declare the rhythm before the beats, as a pattern of quick hits (lowercase), big moments (UPPERCASE) and holds:

- `hook-hook-TURN-proof-proof-PROOF-hold-SIGNOFF` (promo)
- `slow-build-BUILD-PEAK-breathe-signoff` (cinematic)
- `hit-hit-hit-hit-HOLD` (social staccato)

Rules:
- One peak, placed around 60–75% of the runtime, on the strongest music section.
- At least one deliberate held frame where nothing but ambience moves and a line lands.
- The end card holds at least 1.5s (2–3s for a URL the viewer must read).

## 5. Bans list

Write 3–6 project-specific "do nots". Always include the two classic failures:
- **Slideshow:** every beat is a fresh card that appears, holds and is replaced; nothing carries across.
- **Screensaver:** motion that says nothing (blobs drifting, particles floating) while the message waits.

Add brand bans (no blue/green/purple, never recolour the logo) and category bans (no floating credit cards, no rising line graph).
